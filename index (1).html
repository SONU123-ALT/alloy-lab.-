3. From iron ore to iron

Iron occurs as oxides. Hematite (Fe₂O₃) is 69.9% iron by mass and magnetite (Fe₃O₄) is 72.4%, calculated from atomic masses. Mined ore is upgraded to concentrates of 55–65% iron and pellets of about 65% iron. Making iron means removing the oxygen, which chemists call reduction.

Inside a blast furnace, top to bottom

Top

Ore, coke and limestone go in. Rising gas leaves at about 200 °C after giving up most of its heat.

Stack

Carbon monoxide strips oxygen from the ore in steps as it sinks. Limestone breaks down into lime and carbon dioxide.

Lower stack

FeO is reduced to metallic iron at temperatures up to about 1,200 °C.

Tuyeres and hearth

Blast air preheated to 900–1,350 °C burns coke. Gas near the tuyeres reaches about 2,200 °C. Molten iron and slag collect and are tapped at about 1,500 °C.

Reactions

2C + O₂ → 2CO coke burns to carbon monoxide.
Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ overall reduction of hematite.
FeO + CO → Fe + CO₂ final step to metal.
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ limestone decomposes.
CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ lime binds silica into slag.

What comes out

Pig iron (hot metal) holds about 4–5% carbon, plus silicon, manganese, sulfur and phosphorus. That is why it is hard and brittle. Slag typically contains 30–40% SiO₂, 35–45% CaO, 5–15% Al₂O₃ and 5–15% MgO, and floats on the iron.

4. From iron to steel

Steelmaking lowers carbon from about 4–5% down to the grade’s target, for example 0.15–0.20% in 1018, and sets the alloying elements.

After tapping, a ladle furnace adjusts chemistry. Manganese, silicon, chromium, nickel and others are added to land inside the grade’s limits, such as the ones in the library above.

6. Before you build

Melting metal is dangerous. Wear a face shield, leather gloves, natural-fibre clothes and closed leather boots. Keep everything that touches molten metal completely dry, because trapped moisture turns to steam and can throw metal. Work outdoors or with strong ventilation.
Zinc boils at 907 °C, which is lower than the temperature where brass is fully liquid (about 955–965 °C). Brass melting makes zinc fume. Breathing it causes metal fume fever. Add zinc late, keep the melt as cool as you can, and ventilate well.
Steel and stainless melt at 1,400–1,500 °C and are tapped near 1,650 °C in industry. That is far beyond a simple hobby furnace. For steel parts, buy bar stock of the grade and machine or forge it instead.

These specifications are for learning and planning. For anything that carries load or touches food or water, buy material with a mill certificate and follow the relevant standard.
